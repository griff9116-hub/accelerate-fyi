Europe's startup ecosystem has never been stronger. Berlin, Paris, Amsterdam, Stockholm, and Tallinn are producing world-class companies — and the accelerators and investors backing them have gone from regional curiosities to global players. If you're a founder building a tech startup, you now have more European options than ever before.

This guide covers how European accelerators work, what sets them apart from UK programmes, the major hubs and their standout programmes, and how to decide where to apply.

## What Makes European Accelerators Different

European accelerators have a few characteristics that distinguish them from their US and UK counterparts.

**Sector depth over breadth.** Many top European accelerators have carved out specific niches: Startup Wise Guys (B2B SaaS in the Baltics/CEE), IndieBio London (biotech), YES!Delft (deeptech), Climate KIC (climate tech). The best European programme for your startup is often one with deep domain expertise in your sector — not just the biggest name.

**Lower equity, longer relationships.** European programmes typically take less equity than US accelerators (3–8% vs 6–10% for YC). They also tend to build longer-term investor relationships rather than a single Demo Day event. Programmes like Seedcamp and Creandum stay involved through multiple rounds.

**Government and public funding overlap.** Unlike the US, European startup support is often layered: an EU grant from Bpifrance or EIC Accelerator can sit alongside a private accelerator programme. Many founders take multiple sources of non-dilutive capital in parallel.

**Language and legal diversity.** Running a European company means choosing a jurisdiction. Many founders incorporate in the UK (for legal clarity and investor familiarity), Estonia (e-Residency, easy EU access), or the Netherlands (holding company structures). Your accelerator choice can influence this — some programmes strongly prefer locally incorporated companies.

## The Major Hubs

### London

Europe's largest startup hub by deal volume and the natural home for English-speaking founders. Key programmes:

- **Entrepreneur First** — The world's best pre-team accelerator. EF recruits individual talented people and helps them form co-founding teams. Takes ~7% for a stipend + investment. If you don't have a co-founder yet, EF is worth considering before anything else.
- **Seedcamp** — The original European seed fund, now investing €100k–€500k across Europe. More VC than accelerator, but with a strong community.
- **Antler UK** — Global pre-idea programme. Invests £120k for ~10% in 6 months. Strong network, no idea required at entry.
- **Techstars London** — Part of the global Techstars network. $20k investment + $100k convertible, 3 months, 6% equity. Strong corporate partner connections.
- **Bethnal Green Ventures** — The go-to for tech-for-good founders. SEIS-eligible, £30k for 6%, excellent for impact-driven startups.

### Berlin

Germany's startup capital and increasingly the most international city in Europe for tech founders.

- **Entrepreneur First Berlin** — Same model as London, targeting German-speaking and European talent.
- **APX** — Backed by Axel Springer and Porsche, APX invests €50k for 5% at the pre-seed stage. Strong media and automotive connections.
- **Project A** — An operational VC that deploys €5m–€20m at Series A, with a full in-house team (engineers, marketers, data scientists) that portfolio companies can access.
- **Cherry Ventures** — One of Berlin's best seed funds, having backed Gorillas, Taxfix, and others. Invests €500k–€3m.
- **High-Tech Gründerfonds (HTGF)** — Germany's largest early-stage investor, funded partly by the German government. Invests €1m at seed, specialising in deeptech and scientific spinouts.

### Paris

France's startup scene has transformed since 2017. The Station F effect is real.

- **Station F** — The world's largest startup campus, based in a refurbished railway station. Home to 1,000+ startups and 30+ programmes including Facebook, Founders Program, Microsoft Ventures, and many sector-specific tracks. The residency itself is affordable (€500–1,500/month for a desk) and equity-free for the campus access — specific programmes within Station F may take equity.
- **Kima Ventures** — One of the most active seed investors in Europe, writing €150k cheques at pre-seed/seed with a fast 2-week decision process.
- **Bpifrance** — France's national investment bank. Provides grants, convertible loans, and equity investment for French startups at every stage. If you're incorporating in France, engaging with Bpifrance early is essential.
- **Partech** — Pan-European VC with a strong presence in Paris, investing from seed to growth.

### Amsterdam

A small but punchy ecosystem, particularly strong in fintech, sustainability, and logistics.

- **Rockstart** — One of the oldest European accelerators, with vertical tracks in agri/food, energy, and digital health. Takes equity, provides mentorship and a global network.
- **YES!Delft** — Based at TU Delft, Europe's top technical university. Focuses on deeptech and hardware startups with strong IP. Equity-free incubation.
- **Peak Capital** — Leading Dutch seed VC, investing €500k–€3m in B2B tech.

### Stockholm and the Nordics

The Nordics punch well above their weight — home to Spotify, Klarna, King, Mojang, and many other global companies.

- **Creandum** — One of Europe's best seed funds, an early backer of Spotify, iZettle, and Klarna. Invests €500k–€5m.
- **EQT Ventures** — Part of the EQT private equity group. Uses AI (Motherbrain) to identify investments. Invests from seed to Series B+.
- **Northzone** — Invested in Spotify, Klarna, and Trustpilot. Primarily Series A–C with some seed.

### Tallinn and the Baltics

Estonia has built the most founder-friendly startup environment in the world, from e-Residency to the highest unicorn density per capita.

- **Startup Wise Guys** — The leading B2B SaaS accelerator in CEE/Baltics. Invests €20k for 8%, with a strong alumni network across Eastern Europe.
- **Tera Ventures** — Tallinn-based VC backing B2B tech startups across CEE/Nordics.
- **Karma Ventures** — Focuses on deeptech and hardware startups in the Baltic/Nordic region.

## Pan-European Programmes

Some of the most powerful options operate across borders.

**EIC Accelerator** (European Innovation Council): Grants of up to €2.5m plus optional equity investment for breakthrough innovations. Open to companies from all EU member states plus associated countries. Competitive but transformative for deeptech and deep science companies. Apply via the EIC portal — the process is lengthy but worth it for the right company.

**Speedinvest**: A Vienna-based pan-European VC investing €1m–€5m at seed, with offices across Europe and sector-specific funds (fintech, marketplaces, health, deeptech). One of the most active seed investors on the continent.

**Antler**: Now operating in 25+ cities globally including London, Berlin, and Amsterdam. The same pre-idea model everywhere — they invest in people, not ideas. Strong for first-time founders who want structure.

## How to Choose

**Match sector depth first.** A biotech founder should prioritise IndieBio or HTGF over a generalist programme, even if the generalist programme is more famous. Sector-specialist investors add more value and make better introductions.

**Consider your target market.** If you're building for German enterprise customers, a Berlin-based accelerator gives you proximity to those buyers. If you're building a pan-European consumer product, London's investor base has more experience with that.

**Check the equity terms carefully.** European equity terms vary more than US terms. Some programmes take 2–3%, others take 10–12%. Run the maths on your expected valuation — a higher-quality programme with 8% equity can be worth more than a lower-quality one with 3%.

**Don't overlook the alumni network.** The single most valuable thing an accelerator provides is warm introductions — to investors, to customers, to future hires. Ask the programme for a list of alumni and reach out to 5–10 of them before you apply.

**Consider stacking.** Many European founders stack a non-dilutive grant (EU funding, national innovation grants) with a seed investment from an accelerator or VC. This extends your runway without additional dilution.

## Applying

Most European accelerators accept rolling applications or have 2–3 cohorts per year. Key tips:

- Apply to 3–5 programmes simultaneously — rejection rates are 90%+ at the best programmes
- Research what each programme values: EF cares about raw talent, Seedcamp about market size, BGV about social impact
- Founder backgrounds matter at the pre-seed stage — lean into your domain expertise
- Have a clear answer to "why now?" — what has changed that makes this the right moment for this company
