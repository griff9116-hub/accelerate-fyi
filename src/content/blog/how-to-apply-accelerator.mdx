## Why most applications fail at the first stage

Accelerator application reviewers read hundreds of applications per cohort. Most rejections happen in the first 60 seconds — not because the idea is bad, but because the application doesn't communicate clearly and quickly.

The goal of the written application is simple: **get an interview**. Not to explain everything, not to tell your full story — just to create enough curiosity and confidence to merit a conversation.

## The 8 questions you'll always face

### 1. "What does your company do?" (30 words max)

This is the hardest question because it forces clarity you may not yet have.

**Template:** *[Company name] is a [product type] that helps [specific customer] [do something / solve specific problem]. We do this by [key mechanism].*

**Bad:** "We're building an AI-powered platform for the future of enterprise communication."
**Good:** "Kora is a Slack integration that automatically summarises meeting notes and assigns action items. We save ops teams 3 hours per week."

### 2. "What's the problem you're solving?"

Be concrete. Use a specific person in a specific situation.

**Bad:** "Enterprise communication is broken and inefficient."
**Good:** "Operations managers at 50-200 person companies spend 6+ hours per week manually writing meeting summaries and chasing owners of action items. This slows decision-making and creates accountability gaps."

### 3. "Why now?"

This is asking: why does this company need to exist *today*, not 5 years ago or 5 years from now? Good answers reference:
- A regulatory change
- A technology shift (LLMs, new APIs)
- A market shift (remote work, cost pressures)
- A distribution unlock

### 4. "What's your traction?"

Be honest and frame what you have. Any signal is better than nothing.

Strong signals (in order): revenue > paid pilots > signed LOIs > active users > waitlist > user interviews showing willingness to pay

If you have nothing yet: "We've spoken to 40 ops managers at companies with 50-500 employees. 35 of 40 confirmed this is a top-3 problem. 12 expressed willingness to pay £200/month."

### 5. "Who is on the team?"

Focus on *why you* for this problem, not your credentials.

**Bad:** "Jane has a CS degree from Oxford and 5 years at Google."
**Good:** "Jane ran operations at two Series A startups, experiencing this exact problem. Tom built 3 products used by 100k+ users. Together, they've lived on both sides of this problem."

### 6. "What's the business model?"

Name it clearly: SaaS subscription, marketplace fee, usage-based, transactional. Give a number: "£150/seat/month, targeting 10-seat SMBs for £18,000 ARR per customer."

### 7. "What are you asking for?"

Be specific about what you want from the programme — not just money, but specific introductions, expertise, or help with particular challenges.

### 8. "Why us specifically?"

Research the accelerator. Mention partners by name. Reference portfolio companies that are relevant. Show you've done your homework.

## The meta-advice

1. **Get it reviewed by someone outside your bubble.** If your mum doesn't understand what you do, rewrite it.
2. **Every sentence should earn its place.** Cut ruthlessly.
3. **Numbers > adjectives.** "Significant growth" means nothing. "3x month-over-month" means something.
4. **Apply earlier than you think.** Most accelerators do rolling reviews and fill spots throughout the process.
