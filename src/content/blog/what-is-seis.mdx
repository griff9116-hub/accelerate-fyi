## What is SEIS?

The Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme (SEIS) is a UK government programme designed to encourage investment into early-stage companies by offering substantial tax reliefs to investors.

When an investor puts money into a SEIS-eligible company, they can claim:
- **50% income tax relief** on investments up to £200,000 per tax year
- **Capital gains tax exemption** on profits from SEIS shares
- **Loss relief** if the investment fails

This makes SEIS investment significantly more attractive than investing in a non-eligible company. A £100,000 SEIS investment effectively costs the investor only £50,000 after tax relief.

## What does SEIS eligibility mean for your startup?

To qualify for SEIS, your company must:
- Be based in the UK
- Have been trading for less than 3 years
- Have gross assets under £350,000 before the investment
- Have fewer than 25 employees
- Not be operating in certain excluded sectors (e.g. property development, banking)
- Not have previously issued EIS shares (though you can use SEIS first, then EIS)

SEIS allows you to raise up to **£250,000** in total under the scheme.

## Why it matters when choosing an accelerator

Many UK accelerators are SEIS-eligible, which means the investment they make in your startup qualifies for these tax reliefs. This is a significant advantage because:

1. **Lower effective dilution** — investors are effectively paying less out of pocket, so they're more willing to invest at the same valuation
2. **Easier fundraising** — SEIS-eligible deals are far easier to close with angel investors
3. **Competitive advantage** — being SEIS-eligible signals that you're a genuine early-stage company

When comparing accelerators, check whether their investment is **SEIS-eligible**. Accelerate.fyi shows this clearly on every listing.

## SEIS vs EIS

The Enterprise Investment Scheme (EIS) is the bigger sibling to SEIS:

| | SEIS | EIS |
|---|---|---|
| Max investment per investor | £200k/year | £1m/year |
| Income tax relief | 50% | 30% |
| Max company raise | £250k | £12m |
| Company age limit | 3 years | 7 years (10 for knowledge-intensive) |

Many companies start with SEIS and then move to EIS for later rounds. Some accelerators offer both.

## How to check if you're eligible

The quickest way is to use [HMRC's SEIS advance assurance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/venture-capital-schemes-apply-for-the-seed-enterprise-investment-scheme) process. This gives you a letter confirming your company should qualify before you raise.

Most good startup solicitors can prepare and submit this on your behalf. It typically takes 4-8 weeks.
